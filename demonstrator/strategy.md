# Strategy card v1 (2026-10-01)

The driver gives this page to every call, unchanged within a batch, so similar piles get similar moves. It condenses
`demonstrator/instructions.md` and `docs/knowledge/mechanical-search-learnings.md`; numbers come from those.

The aim of every move: a red that the front camera sees well, with one jaw axis free at both ends, then pick it.
When two moves look equally good, take the one that comes first below.

1. Know where the red is before you try to grasp it. Picks on a red whose top was well seen lifted it 22 times in
   39; on a roughly placed one (box fit or wrist-only), 1 in 11. While the red is rough, the move's job is to show
   it to the front camera: take a cover off its top, or push a cube or the red itself so its top comes into view.
2. If the red isn't listed, the red pixels say where it is. In every buried episode so far it was under or just
   behind the cube at the red pixels, within 1.5 cm. Take that cube off first.
3. Covers on the red come off first. It's the cheapest progress.
4. Then work one jaw axis, the one with fewer blockers. Both ends need room: the fixed finger lands 4 to 14 mm outside
   one face, the open moving finger 20 to 28 mm outside the other. Under stiff contacts, clearing one end is not
   enough.
5. A blocker can be pushed (one move) or picked and placed (two moves). Pushing a blocker sideways off the axis
   worked 19 times in 38 when the far end of that axis was already clear, and 38 in 194 otherwise. Neighbours tend
   to move together, the red included; look after every move and don't assume the red stayed put.
6. Once act allows a pick of a well-seen red, pick it. A push beside a pickable red kept it pickable 10 times in 22.
7. Spills end the episode. Don't drive a cube at layer 2 or higher toward a wall (act refuses within 15 mm); keep
   strokes short (5 cm did as well as 8); layer-3 cubes near a wall sit above the rim and go over easily.
8. Count moves: 15 at most. A cover costs 1 move (sweep) or 2 (pick and place), a blocker 1 (push) or 2.
9. Recover from a miss or a bad push with a move that follows from what the cameras now show. Don't repeat a move
   that just failed in the same scene.
