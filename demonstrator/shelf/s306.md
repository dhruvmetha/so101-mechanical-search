## Lessons

1. Before clearing anything, map the red pick's swept band and count the free spots you can reach outside it.
   This gripper's level pick cuts centres about 5 cm left and 4.5 cm right of the ray, so every gray within that
   band needs a destination with its own clear lane. In a packed bay that count decides the episode before the
   first pick. Here it was one marginal spot for two grays, so no plan could work.
2. With pushes off, some hidden starts can't be solved by pick and place alone. A packed front row locks itself,
   because each gray needs about 3 cm free on both sides to be picked, and a gray within about 4 cm of the right
   wall can't be picked at all. Reset accepts such a start, since its free-spot rule doesn't ask whether the spot
   can be reached. A reset check for pick-and-place solvability (reachable free spots versus grays in the red's
   band), or a push that doesn't topple, would stop the expert from being handed dead episodes.

Tool note: the lowest pixel of a base ellipse is the base point nearest the camera along its optical axis, not
along the sight line. Stepping one radius along the sight line put cylinders at the image edges 0.7 to 1.1 cm off.
Stepping along the optical axis made the two looks agree within 4 mm.
