## Lessons

1. On this shelf, a hidden red near the right wall always comes with a gray at its front-right: the front camera sits at y = -0.30, and the hidden start puts a gray on its sight line. That gray can't be picked (its moving jaw would hit the wall), and it can't be pushed left. Push it straight back along the wall from its front, heading 0, starting from the rightmost start the shelf-path check accepts. With pushes allowed that worked twice without a topple. But the gripper's left side drags the red back with it, so the red ends up deeper and against whatever sits behind it. Plan the pushes knowing the red goes back too, and clear the grays on its left (here C and D) as part of that plan, not afterwards.
2. Re-deproject from every new wrist view before each risky move, and check a pick against ghosts from more than one view. The base-lowest estimate moved J by 1 cm and R by 1 cm between views, which is the whole margin in a packed back corner. One view also held a ghost cylinder that the next view showed was empty board. That ghost had made D look unpickable.

Tool note: nothing broke. Every clearance check here is kinematic against my own ghosts, and ghost error (3 to 10 mm) was bigger than most margins. A pick with a predicted 5 mm cut at grasp height went through without a topple, but I wouldn't bet on that across the friction range.
