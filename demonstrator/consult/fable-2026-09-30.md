# Consult: making the expert demonstrator produce good demos, fast

Fable, 2026-09-30. Read: CLAUDE.md, demonstrator/REPORT.md, RELATED_WORK.md, instructions.md (v9), AGENT_BRIEF.md,
tools.py, perception.py, export.py, postmortem.py, the episode notes, the task spec, and the primitive env in
so101sim/mechsearch.py. I ran nothing but greps and a count over the episode files.

## The baseline, as numbers

- 36 episodes on file, 303 acts, 179 parks, 16 undos. 26 solved and exported. 13 acts have no `unpark` flag and 14
  no `flip`, so they replay under the old behaviour.
- About 38 agent requests and 4.8M cached tokens per episode (1,135 requests, 144M tokens, 30 episodes). 15 to 20 min
  of wall clock per episode per agent. Eight agents hit the session limit at 02:33.
- Where the wall clock goes: every tool call rebuilds the episode by replaying it, 5 to 30 s (AGENT_BRIEF.md). At
  roughly four calls per move (observe, two checks, act) and 10 moves, replay alone is 5 to 15 min of each episode.
  `check` also plans both wrist rolls over the whole path each time.
- Where the moves go: 80% shuttle grays, 12 of 128 picks lifted nothing, 5 of 29 red lifts came up tilted and cost two
  moves each, 21% of places moved three or more other cubes, parking is 14% of robot time.
- Physics cost is what will bound a scripted planner. I have no measured number for one primitive. Reset takes 0.5 s
  buried, which suggests about 1 ms per physics step with noslip on. A 4 s primitive plus settle is then about 2 s
  of CPU. Measure this first; every estimate below scales with it.

The plan's core idea is right: a scripted planner that proposes from perception and tests on the sim, with Claude only
as a fallback. What is wrong is the order and the scale. The tree is built before the planner that would use it, the
perturbations test chaos instead of the errors that matter, and the sweep study is scheduled before the two changes
that remove most wasted moves. My ordering below puts the planner first and the tree last.

## Prioritised list

### 1. Keep the env alive and act on snapshots; stop replaying per call

What: a `mujoco.MjData` copy plus the `Episode` bookkeeping (`robot_s`, `last`, `op_index`, the env's `_streak` and
`_n`) is the snapshot. The planner runs in one process, snapshots before each candidate, restores after. `tools.py`
gets a long-lived server mode (or a REPL) so a Claude rescue does not rebuild the episode per call either. Run
episodes as one process each across the 32 cores.

Why: replay is the largest single wall-clock cost, and it grows with episode length. Undo is also free once restore
exists; today it is a JSON edit plus a full replay.

Done when: restoring a snapshot and running the next op gives bit-identical `result` fields and `qpos` to replaying
from the start, tested over every op in all 36 episode files. A 10-move episode's planner loop spends under 5% of its
time outside physics and rendering.

Effort: 1 day. Gain: for a Claude-driven episode, wall clock drops from 15 to 20 min to about 5 to 8 (the model's own
latency stays). For the scripted planner it is the difference between 13 min and hours per episode, see item 2.

### 2. Build the depth-1 planner now, with the sim as the only judge

What: the rulebook v9 is already a program. Code it as a candidate generator over the perception in `Episode.see()`:
cover removal from the top, the red-line and bridging-cube locators, clearing one jaw axis, the wall and height
rules. Each candidate is run on a snapshot; the planner commits the best by outcome and logs the intent string with
the move, the way agents wrote "why". Perception proposes, the sim disposes. True poses are used only to score what
happened, never to generate a candidate, so every kept move has a visible reason.

Why: this is the item that changes both goals at once. Every kept move is one that worked, so the kept line has no
empty picks (12 of 128 today), no tilted red lifts, no spills and no undos, by construction. And it removes Claude
from the routine moves, which are most of them: 80% of moves are shuttles that rules already decide.

Cube flag: code the loop's structure (locate the target, find what rests on it, clear the grasp's swept volume,
relocate blockers, grasp), and keep every cube number in one object model that the generator asks. The rulebook's
"yaw or yaw + 90", "clear both ends of one jaw axis" and "42 mm" are cube facts, not planner facts. The section
below lists them. The lookahead itself is the one object-agnostic part of this design, which is a second reason to
build it before codifying more rules: on YCB objects the rules will be wrong and the sim will still say so.

Done when: the planner alone solves at least the agents' rate on seeds 1000 to 1034 (agents: 24 of 25 finished, with
undos), and the kept line of every solved episode has zero empty picks and zero undos. Postmortem's "lifted nothing"
count is the check. A second check: the cube model is one module, and grepping the planner for `CUBE`, `HALF` and
`LAYER` finds nothing outside it.

Effort: 3 to 4 days for the loop plus the first two candidate families (covers, axis clearing), a day more for walls
and leaning cubes. Gain: with 8 candidates per move and one copy each, a 10-move episode is about 160 s of physics
at 2 s per candidate, so 20 episodes per core-hour. Across 24 cores that is hundreds per hour, against 6 last night,
at zero tokens. Even if the planner only solves half the seeds alone, cost per demo falls by more than 5x.

### 3. Make "relocate" one decision, with the spot chosen by code

What: a relocate candidate is a pick of a named cube plus a placement the planner picks from `places`, screened for the
things `places` misses today: leaning neighbours, layer-2 tops, spots beside the red's neighbours (instructions.md,
"Clearing"). It is still a pick and a place in the trajectory; it is one decision and one lookahead.

Why: places are 41% of moves and the more disruptive half (21% moved three or more cubes, against 14% of picks).
Three episodes lost later picks to a hand-chosen spot beside a neighbour. With lookahead, the spot is tested before
it is committed, so the disturbance is measured rather than guessed.

Cube flag: "the top of a well-seen layer-1 cube" is the cube version of a spot. The general spot is a patch of the
heightmap at least the held object's footprint wide, flat within a tolerance, away from the target's support set,
and confirmed by lookahead (the object stays where it was put after settling). `spots()` in tools.py is already most
of that; `places` is not, since it walks the cube list.

Done when: decisions per solved buried episode drop from a median of 10.5 to about 6, and the share of relocations
that move three or more other cubes falls below 10% in postmortem.

Effort: 1 day on top of item 2. Gain: 40% fewer decisions, which means 40% fewer candidate evaluations and, when
Claude is involved, 40% fewer requests. Robot time is unchanged except for the park it removes between pick and place.

### 4. Lift the red higher on the final pick, and add a regrasp candidate

What: the pick lifts to `travel_z`, which leaves about 2 mm to spare for a level cube (instructions.md, "Grasping").
Give the red's pick a higher final lift, inside the reach limit above z = 0.10 that REPORT.md notes is untested. Add a
"set down level and regrasp" candidate for a red that reads holding but tilted.

Why: 5 of 29 red lifts came up short and each cost two moves. That is about 0.4 wasted moves per episode for a
one-line change in the primitive.

Cube flag: the success line is "lowest corner 2 cm above where a top-layer cube rests" (mechsearch.py,
`_lowest_corner`). The general line is the target's lowest surface point, from its mesh, a margin above the rim or
above the pile's highest point at reset. Fix that when the lift height changes, since both live in the same check.

Done when: a red held at 15 deg of tilt counts, and the 5 tilted lifts from the notes (e400 move 9, d1004, d1000
move 15 among them) would have passed. Effort: half a day. Gain: about 4% of all moves.

### 5. Score under perturbations at the scale that matters, with a spill veto

What: the plan's 3 copies at 1 micrometre test chaos. What a demo has to survive is perception error: 0.6 mm median
for trusted poses, about 4 mm for wrist-only ones, 1 in 10 box fits off by up to 16 mm, and the red-line locator
within 1.5 cm (tools.py, instructions.md). Perturb the candidate's aim by those amounts and the pile by a few mm,
re-settle with `rest.come_to_rest` so the copies start at rest, and use 5 to 8 copies. Accept a move only if no copy
spills and most copies reach the intended outcome. Keep the intended outcome explicit (cube lifted, cover off the red,
red uncovered) so the score is about the move's purpose, not a proxy.

Why: this is what makes kept moves "make sense on their own". A move that works only at the exact aim is a lucky
move, and lookahead over one exact copy would keep it. Two of three copies cannot tell 50% from 90%.

Done when: a calibration table over 200 committed moves shows realised outcome within 10 points of the score's
prediction, and the spill rate in kept lines is zero. Effort: 1 to 2 days after item 1. Cost: it multiplies physics
per move by the copy count, so run it at 5 copies and only on candidates that passed one exact copy.

### 6. Instrument cost and throughput, and gate demos by number

What: per episode, log wall clock, physics seconds, renders, candidates evaluated, Claude requests and tokens, robot
time, decisions, and postmortem's disturbance and empty-pick counts. Define "good" as numbers and let `export.py`
refuse anything outside them: zero empty picks, zero undos in the kept line, robot time under a cap, no move that
disturbed more than N cubes without a stated reason.

Why: without this nothing on this list can be shown to have helped. Last night's numbers came from a post-hoc script.
The 26 demos already include e402, which REPORT.md says to drop for cleanliness, and the pre-v6 episodes played
without the wrist camera.

Done when: `python demonstrator/report.py` prints demos per hour, cost per demo and the gate's pass rate per batch,
and demos.json carries the per-episode numbers. Effort: 1 day.

### 7. Freeze, commit and stamp versions; test the perception and the planner

What: the repo has no commits at all (`git log` is empty) and a demo replays exactly only under the same code and
physics (CLAUDE.md). Commit now, and write the commit hash, MuJoCo version (3.8.1), rulebook version and primitive
version into every episode file and demos.json. Then tests: synthetic piles with known poses through `see()`, the
hits-against-outcome regression on the 128 recorded picks (the 96/80/75% table in instructions.md), `axis_ends` and
`places` on constructed cases, and a golden replay of three episodes. Also raise the model's arena memory:
MUJOCO_LOG.TXT holds one "Insufficient arena memory" warning from 00:02 last night, which means MuJoCo dropped
constraints on that step.

Why: the tools changed under running agents and one rule was written backwards for hours; nobody could tell early from
late demos without reading the changelog. A planner that codifies the rules will carry any such bug into every demo
it makes, silently and fast.

Done when: `pytest -q -m "not mjx"` covers perception and the planner, the arena warning cannot be reproduced over 40
resets, and every episode file names its versions. Effort: 1 to 2 days.

### 8. Define "stuck" and make the Claude rescue cheap

What: stuck means no candidate passes item 5's rule after the generator is exhausted, or the move budget says the plan
cannot finish. The rescue hands Claude a compact state: the red's estimate, the cubes over it and on its axes, the
candidates tried and why they failed, and the image. Not the full 30-cube list. One episode per agent, at most 4
agents, a hard cap of about 10 requests per rescue, and the agent's moves go through the same lookahead so a rescue
cannot put a miss into the kept line.

Why: 38 requests and 4.8M tokens per episode came from Claude doing every routine move with a growing context and a
full observe printout each time. REPORT.md's item 6 already lists most of this.

Done when: requests per demo average under 5 across a 40-seed batch and no batch hits a session limit. Effort:
1 day. Gain: cost per demo from about 4.8M tokens to a few hundred thousand on rescued episodes and zero on the rest.

### 9. Look from the hover pose instead of parking, if perception holds

What: after each primitive the arm is at `travel_z` over its last target. Measure `see()` from there on the six
calibration piles against the parked numbers (0.6 mm median, 38% unseen). If it is close, the planner observes from
hover and never parks.

Why: parking is 14% of robot time, and the park and unpark motions are where two of the early failures came from
(e400's drop, e401's 93 deg roll lag). Fewer motions is also a cleaner kept line.

Done when: a table of pose error and unseen fraction from hover against parked, and a decision. Effort: half a day
to measure, half a day to switch. Gain: up to 14% of robot time per episode.

### 10. Treat the sweep as one more candidate type, and study strokes, not episodes

What: give push its stroke length and height (plan item 4, keep it). Add rim and layer-2 sweeps to the generator with
the corridor rule from RELATED_WORK.md, under item 5's spill veto. For the rule itself, run a few hundred random
strokes on random piles from snapshots and log layer, wall distance, heading and outcome. Skip the 40-seed
episode-level sweep-versus-pick study until the planner exists; the planner's logs will show which candidate type won
each move for free.

Why: the pilot's 8 of 16 clears came aimed with true poses; 2 of 16 spilled, and a spill ends a demo. Covers are
about 4 of the 10.5 median moves and sweeps cannot open the floor, so the ceiling is about a third of the moves.
Relocate and lookahead remove more waste with no new physics risk, so they go first.

Cube flag: "layer-2 centre height" and "8 mm above the red's spot" are cube heights. The general stroke height is
read off the heightmap under the corridor: a set fraction of the way up whatever sits over the target's estimated
footprint. Fit the corridor rule on heightmap features (height of the swept material, distance to the wall), not
on layer numbers, or it has to be refitted for every object set.

Done when: a stroke table with spill and clear rates per heading and layer, and a corridor rule fitted to it with
under 2% predicted spill. Effort: 2 days. Gain: where a sweep wins, one stroke replaces two relocations, about 8 s of
robot time.

### 11. Change what the move cap counts

What: 15 primitives was set for a red under a two-cube stack (spec). Buried starts need 7 to 13 and d1000 needed 16.
With relocate as one decision the count changes meaning anyway. Cap on robot time (say 120 s) or on decisions, and
record both.

Why: a cap that the median buried start nearly fills turns the last moves into gambles, which is where d1000's tilted
lift came from. Effort: an hour. Gain: fewer episodes lost at move 14.

## What only works for 30 mm cubes

The bin is a proof of concept and the same expert is meant to work on YCB or similar objects later. Here is what in
the current tools and plan assumes a 30 mm cube, where it lives, and what the object-agnostic version is. The short
version: the perception, the grasp rules and the placement rules are cube-specific; the clearance check, the
heightmap, the snapshot lookahead and the perturbation scoring are not. So the plan's investment should go to the
second group, and the first group should be one swappable module.

| Cube assumption | Where | Object-agnostic version |
|---|---|---|
| Pose from a bounding square on the top face, coverage as extent over 30 mm (`top_face_pose`), and a 30 mm box fit for partly seen cubes (`box_pose`, `yaw_and_tilt`) | perception.py | Per-instance segment point cloud with no model fit: a heightmap footprint, a principal-axis heading, and the visible surface's extent. Where a model exists (YCB meshes are known), a mesh pose fit against the visible points, with the fit residual as the trust score in place of "60% of the top seen". |
| Layers at floor + 1.5, 4.5, 7.5 cm; `layer = round((z - floor - half) / 0.03) + 1`; the action box's z runs from layer 1 to layer 3 | tools.py `see()`, mechsearch.py `MechSearchPrimitiveEnv.__init__`, the rulebook, RELATED_WORK's corridor rule | Continuous heights from the heightmap. A grasp's z comes from the segment's visible top minus a grasp depth for that object. The action box's z runs from floor plus the smallest object's grasp height to the highest point reset allows. |
| Two jaw axes at yaw and yaw + 90, both "face pairs"; `axis_ends` looks 30 mm out along strips one cube wide; "grip the upright pair of faces" for leaning cubes | tools.py `axis_ends`, instructions.md "Grasping" | Grasp candidates sampled on the segment: antipodal pairs on the visible surface for a known mesh, or heightmap-based top-down grasps at many headings (VPG-style, 16 headings over the segment's footprint). `axis_ends` becomes "what the fingers' swept volume at this grasp intersects", which `check` already computes on the heightmap for any object. |

The 42 comes from 30 + those margins. The general form is the object's width along the jaw axis at the grasp height plus the two margins, computed per grasp candidate. |

| Cover set "every cube above the red's footprint through the graph"; postmortem's `red_state` uses cube multiples | RELATED_WORK step 3, postmortem.py | A rests-on graph from contacts in teacher mode (any geometry), and from heightmap overlap in deployable mode. `red_state` becomes "objects whose footprint overlaps the target's and whose bottom is above the target's top". |
| The buried reset recipe: red on the floor, four flush grays, one on top; acceptance by face pixels | mechsearch.py reset, spec amendment 2 | The face-pixel label is generic. What survives unchanged: `check`'s finger and mount sampling against the heightmap, `Clearance`, `rest.come_to_rest`,
the snapshot lookahead, the perturbation scoring, the spill veto, the cost instrumentation and the export gate. That
is the part of the plan worth building well. The cube rulebook should be codified only as far as items 2 to 4 need,
behind an interface that a YCB object model can implement without touching the loop.

## What to cut or change in the plan

- Cut the full search tree (branch from any node, goto, kept branches with observation summaries). It is a user
  interface for a Claude that is supposed to be the fallback. Snapshot and restore with a linear "try, then commit"
  loop is what the planner needs. Add depth only if item 2 stalls on the neighbour step in more than about a fifth of
  buried seeds, and even then a depth-2 loop is cheaper than a tree with bookkeeping. RELATED_WORK.md's own order of
  work put snapshot last; I put it first, but thin.
- Change the perturbation scheme as in item 5: perception-scale, 5 to 8 copies, spill veto, an explicit intended
  outcome.
- Defer the 40-seed sweep study behind the planner, relocate and the higher lift. Keep the stroke length and height
  change; that is cheap and the planner needs it.
- Stop running parallel Claude agents against a rulebook that changes under them. Freeze per batch, stamp versions,
  and drop pre-v6 episodes and e402 from any set that claims to be clean.
- Stop scoring demos by "solved". Score by the gate in item 6. A demo with an empty pick in the kept line is not a
  good demo even if it solved the task.
- Don't use the Warp backend for lookahead. CLAUDE.md records that piles creep on the GPU and contact order varies run
  to run, so a candidate that works on the GPU says little about the CPU replay that export checks.

## What would change my mind

- If one primitive costs 10 s of CPU rather than 2, the planner is the bottleneck and the perturbation copies (item 5)
  go down to 3 and the parallel pool becomes the priority. Measure this first.
- If the planner alone solves under half the seeds, Claude is still doing most of the work and the tree becomes worth
  building, because a Claude that can branch and compare will need fewer requests than one that undoes and replays.
- If sweeps under a 1.5 cm aim perturbation keep spills under 2% and clear covers in over half the tries, the sweep
  moves ahead of relocate, since one stroke would then replace two relocations on most buried starts.
- If perception from the hover pose is worse than about 3 mm median or leaves over half the floor unseen, keep parking
  and accept the 14%.
- If the depth-1 planner's kept lines still show more than about 5% of moves disturbing three or more cubes,
  perturbation scoring is not enough and the candidate generator needs a disturbance term, not more copies.
- If a YCB scene is coming within weeks rather than months, skip codifying the cube rulebook past covers and axis
  clearing, and spend the time on the heightmap grasp sampler and the mesh pose fit instead, since those are what the
  planner will run on either object set.
